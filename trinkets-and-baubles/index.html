Trinkets & Baubles

An Unofficial Warhammer Fantasy Battles 8th Edition Expansion · Draft 3

Not every charm carried to war was forged by a master runesmith, stolen from an ancient tomb, or blessed beneath the light of Morrslieb. Most are far humbler things: a smooth stone from a childhood stream, a lover's letter folded until the creases have nearly worn through, an old button, a bent nail, a black feather, a prayer bead, or some other scrap its owner simply refuses to leave behind.

Soldiers are superstitious creatures, and the Old World gives them ample reason to be. A man who survives an arrow through his cap may keep that cap for the rest of his life. A regiment that wins three battles beneath the same tattered ribbon may begin to believe the ribbon itself watches over them. Such beliefs are easy to mock—until the charm seems to work.

In a world where faith can summon miracles and emotion can shape the Winds of Magic, who can say such things are entirely powerless? Perhaps a familiar keepsake simply gives its bearer courage at precisely the right moment. Perhaps years of desperate belief leave the faintest magical resonance. Whatever the truth, the boon is so slight that most wizards would never notice it at all.

Why Trinkets & Baubles?
Army lists do not provide useful purchases at every possible value. Trinkets & Baubles exist to spend the awkward one, two, three or four points that would otherwise be wasted. Their effects are intentionally tiny, narrow and often one-use. They are not meant to be efficient upgrades worth planning around. If players begin restructuring armies specifically to fit more of them, they are probably too good.

General Rules

  • Trinkets & Baubles are purchased with points and assigned to a specific eligible unit.
  • They do not count toward normal Magic Item allowances.
  • Characters cannot carry Trinkets & Baubles.
  • Characters cannot receive their benefits, even while joined to a unit that possesses one.
  • A unit may normally possess no more than 1 Trinket or Bauble.
    • If the army contains 4 or fewer non-Character units, each may carry up to 2.
    • If the army contains 2 or fewer non-Character units, each may carry up to 3.
  • An army may contain no more than 5 Trinkets & Baubles in total.
  • Unless stated otherwise, an item's effect applies only to this unit or an eligible model within it.
  • Daemonic units cannot select the standard Trinkets & Baubles in this draft. Daemonic Resonances are intended as their equivalent.
Soldier's Keepsakes
Eligibility: Core Infantry units only.

The smallest treasures of the rank and file: things carried for confidence, habit, memory or because throwing them away after surviving this long would obviously be tempting fate.

Smooth River Stone 1 pt

A perfectly ordinary stone polished by years of nervous thumbs.

Once per game, one rank-and-file model in this unit may re-roll a natural 1 To Hit in close combat.

Broken Whetstone 1 pt

Far too worn to sharpen much of anything, though its owner insists otherwise.

Once per game, one rank-and-file model in this unit may re-roll a natural 1 To Wound in close combat.

Copper Button 1 pt

From an old uniform whose original owner is remembered as being terribly quick.

Once per game, one rank-and-file model in this unit may gain +1 Initiative for a single Close Combat phase.

Three Knots of Twine 1 pt

One knot for luck, one against witches, and one because nobody remembers what the third was for.

Once per game, one rank-and-file model in this unit may gain +1 to a single characteristic when taking a characteristic test, to a maximum of 10. Declare before rolling.

Brass Buckle 2 pts

Its owner claims it once turned aside a blade. The dent certainly looks convincing.

Once per game, one rank-and-file model in this unit may receive +1 to its armour save against a single Wound. Declare before rolling the save.

Charcoal Blade-Mark 2 pts

A black line drawn along the weapon's edge according to an old camp superstition.

Once per game, one rank-and-file model in this unit may gain +1 Strength for a single close combat attack.

Bent Arrowhead 2 pts

Dug out of something—or someone—after a previous battle and kept for luck.

Once per game, one rank-and-file model in this unit may re-roll a natural 1 To Hit with a single missile attack.

Sergeant's False Tooth 4 pts

Passed from regiment to regiment. Biting down on it is said to put some real spite into a swing.

Once per game, one rank-and-file model in this unit gains +1 Attack for a single Close Combat phase.

Wayfarer's Tokens
Eligibility: Non-Character, non-Monster units.

Roadside finds, worn travelling pieces and little habits soldiers swear make the next mile shorter or the dangerous ground a little kinder.

Old Boot Nail 1 pt

A bent nail supposedly taken from the boot of a soldier who marched home from three wars.

The first time this unit takes a Leadership test in order to march while within 8" of an enemy, it gains +1 Leadership for that test.

Charmed Horseshoe 2 pts

Too small for a horse, but that has never stopped anyone carrying it.

Once per game, one rank-and-file model in this unit may re-roll a failed Dangerous Terrain test.

Wayfarer's Pebble 3 pts

A little piece of the road ahead, carried in the hope that the rest of it comes more quickly.

The first time this unit declares a charge, add +1" to its charge distance. This Trinket has no effect on a unit with Swiftstride.

Hare's Foot 3 pts

Whether it was lucky for the hare remains a matter of debate.

The first time this unit pursues an enemy, add +1" to its pursuit distance. This Trinket has no effect on a unit with Swiftstride.

Scout's Chalk 3 pts

Used to mark doors, roads, trees, corpses and anything else somebody might need to find again.

Once per game, this unit may add +1" to a normal move or march move. This cannot be used when charging, fleeing or pursuing.

Black Feather 4 pts

Taken from a bird that supposedly escaped every trap ever laid for it.

Once per game, when this unit flees, add +1" to its flee distance. This Trinket has no effect on a unit with Swiftstride.

Keepsakes, Charms & Tiny Acts of Fortune
Tokens of Courage
Eligibility: Non-Character, non-Monster units.

Not every charm promises victory. Some merely remind a soldier why running, surrendering or dying here would be terribly inconvenient.

Folded Love Letter 2 pts

Creased, stained and read often enough that half the words have disappeared.

The first time this unit takes a Fear test, it gains +1 Leadership for that test.

Homeward Coin 3 pts

Never spent. Spending it would imply its owner no longer intends to make it home.

The first time this unit attempts to Rally, it gains +1 Leadership for that test.

Knotted Cord 3 pts

Every knot represents someone who chased a fleeing enemy farther than was wise.

The first time this unit tests to Restrain Pursuit, it gains +1 Leadership for that test.

Little Tin Horn 4 pts

Far too small to make any useful noise, but soldiers swear they can hear it when things begin going wrong.

Once per game, this unit may gain +1 Leadership for a single Panic test. Declare before rolling.

Good Luck Charms
Eligibility: Non-Character, non-Monster units.

Objects credited with one improbable escape are often carried forever afterward. The logic is rarely sophisticated: it stopped a bullet once, survived a fire once, or happened to be present when a curse failed—so surely it can do so again.

Shot-Through Prayer Book 1 pt

Kept in a breast pocket ever since a shot tore through its cover and stopped short of the soldier's heart.

This unit gains a 6+ Ward save against the first Wound caused by missile attacks against a rank-and-file model.

Blackened Tin Spoon 1 pt

It twisted and turned black in a soldier's pocket on the very night a hedge-witch's curse failed to take him.

This unit gains a 6+ Ward save against the first Wound caused by spells against a rank-and-file model.

Sigmarite Wax Seal 2 pts

Broken from a temple writ that survived a sorcerous fire which reduced the rest of the dispatch case to ash.

This unit gains a 5+ Ward save against the first Wound caused by Magical Attacks against a rank-and-file model.

Dented Copper Penny 2 pts

A knife once struck the coin instead of the ribs beneath it. Its owner has refused to spend it ever since.

This unit gains a 5+ Ward save against the first Wound caused by non-magical attacks against a rank-and-file model.

Half-Burnt Wedding Ribbon 3 pts

The wearer escaped a burning house with little else intact. One end of the ribbon still bears the scorch marks.

This unit gains a 4+ Ward save against the first Wound caused by Flaming Attacks against a rank-and-file model.

Peg from a Misfired Gun 3 pts

Taken from an enemy cannon that burst apart just as its crew prepared to fire on the regiment. Nobody calls that coincidence.

This unit gains a 4+ Ward save against the first Wound caused by a War Machine against a rank-and-file model.

Lucky Stitching 4 pts

A few strangely coloured stitches hidden inside a sleeve, collar or shield strap.

Once per game, one rank-and-file Infantry or Cavalry model in this unit may re-roll a failed armour save.

Worn Prayer Bead 4 pts

Rubbed smooth by someone who may not have been entirely certain who they were praying to.

Once per game, one rank-and-file model in this unit may gain a 5+ Ward save against a single Wound. Declare before attempting the save.

Making Your Own: A custom bauble should still feel like something a soldier could actually carry and believe in. Whenever possible, the superstition should have a reason behind it: a prayer book that once caught a bullet makes sense as protection from missiles; a scorched ribbon carried out of a burning home makes sense as protection from flame. The object need not truly be magical—the belief only needs to make sense to the person carrying it.

Design Principle

Categories exist to keep repeated restrictions out of individual item rules and to make an expanding collection easy to browse. Items within each category are presented in ascending points cost rather than divided into separate one-, two-, three- and four-point lists.

The tiny effects remain the heart of the expansion. These are not lesser legendary artefacts. They are the almost-imperceptible boons of habit, sentiment, superstition and perhaps just enough magic to make a soldier swear the old charm worked again.
